Design Direction Comparison
Evaluating directions against core UX principles for web-only MVP.
Direction A: Card-Based ApprovalRecommended
✓ Pre-computed risk summary cards — CFO sees everything at a glance
✓ One-click approve/reject with visual hierarchy
✓ Expandable for detailed review
✓ Risk dots (green/yellow/red) for instant assessment
✓ Metrics grid (BRL equiv, rate, lock window, milestones)
○ Takes more vertical space than table view
→ Best for: CFO approval queue
Direction B: Risk Table
✓ Dense, scannable — power users can process many items fast
✓ Sortable by risk/amount/time
✓ Compact for high-volume review days
○ Risk details require hover expansion
○ Less visual impact for approval moment
→ Best for: CFO on high-volume days
Direction C: Minimal BuyerBest for Carlos
✓ Maximum clarity on what buyer pays and supplier receives
✓ Rate lock celebration creates emotional anchor
✓ Single CTA button — "Confirm Payment"
✓ Progressive disclosure for escrow details
○ No visual hierarchy beyond rate lock moment
→ Best for: Carlos buyer flow

Web-Only MVP Direction

Carlos (Buyer): Direction C's minimal confirmation with Direction A's rate lock celebration. Payment link opens in browser — single page with amount confirmation, rate lock moment, and status tracker. Progressive disclosure reveals escrow details and milestone progress below the fold.

Wei (Seller): Direction A's dense dashboard. Full-screen console with sidebar navigation, KPI cards, sortable settlement table, and prominent "Download Complete Evidence Pack" button. Desktop-first with responsive scaling to 1024px tablets.

CFO (Approver): Direction A's card-based approval queue as primary, Direction B's risk table as optional toggle for power users on high-volume days.

📋 ERP modules cover Sales Ledger, Purchase Ledger, Account Reconciliation, Counterparty Management, and Invoice Creation — as defined in the PRD (FR63–FR87).
